# Computer Systems Summary

## Part 1: Supercomputer vs Mainframe

### Supercomputer
- **Purpose:** Extremely fast calculations for science and research (weather, climate, nuclear/molecular simulations, AI).
- **Architecture:** Massively parallel, with thousands to millions of CPUs/GPUs on custom high-speed networks; usually Linux-based HPC operating systems.
- **Performance:** Measured in FLOPS (petaflops to exaflops).
- **Size and cost:** Fills entire rooms or buildings, needs heavy cooling and power, and costs hundreds of millions of dollars.
- **Users:** Researchers, universities, and government agencies.

### Mainframe
- **Purpose:** High-volume transaction and data processing for banking, insurance, government, and airlines.
- **Architecture:** Centralized and specialized, with a smaller number of powerful processors focused on I/O, reliability, and security; runs proprietary systems like z/OS.
- **Performance:** Measured in MIPS and transactions per second, built to serve thousands of concurrent users.
- **Size and cost:** More compact than supercomputers, with "five nines" (99.999%) uptime; very expensive, but cheaper than supercomputers.
- **Users:** Enterprise IT departments running business-critical applications.

### Shared traits
Both are high-performance, enterprise-grade, built on specialized hardware and software, and costly to build and run.

### In short
Supercomputers solve huge, complex computational problems fast, while mainframes handle massive volumes of simpler tasks with exceptional reliability.

## Part 2: What's Inside a PC System

### Processor (CPU)
The "nerve centre" of the PC, built into a single silicon chip (an integrated circuit) that executes program instructions and coordinates the system. It has three parts:
- **Control unit:** interprets instructions and directs the other components.
- **ALU (arithmetic logic unit):** does maths (+, -) and logic operations (AND, OR, NOT).
- **Registers:** very fast memory units. The program counter tracks the next instruction; the instruction register holds the current one.

### Speed
The system clock sends signals at fixed intervals to synchronize data flow. Clock speed is measured in gigahertz (GHz); 4GHz means four thousand million cycles per second.

### Memory
- **RAM:** volatile, so it loses its contents when the power is off. The CPU copies programs from the hard disk into RAM to run them. It can be expanded with extra chips on circuit boards called DIMMs.
- **ROM:** non-volatile, holding instructions for basic operations. The BIOS uses ROM to control communication with peripherals.

### Buses and cards
- **Motherboard:** the main circuit board, holding the processor, memory chips, expansion slots and peripheral controllers.
- **Buses:** electrical channels that let devices communicate. The front side bus carries all data from the CPU to other devices.
- **Bus width:** how much data a bus can carry at once, like lanes on a motorway (a 64-bit bus carries 64 bits).
- **Expansion slots:** let you install expansion cards that add sound, memory and network capabilities.

## Part 3: AI on Trial (Legal English, C1-C2)

### The reading text: "The age of the machine"
- **Warnings and calls for action (A):** Geoffrey Hinton, the "Godfather of AI", has warned publicly about the dangers of AI. A thousand experts, including Elon Musk, signed an open letter calling for a pause on large-scale projects such as new chatbots. Pleas for regulation have led to government hearings worldwide, with some suggesting global treaties like those for nuclear weapons. Historians compare generative AI to the industrial revolution or the atomic bomb.
- **Early history (B):** Machine intelligence emerged in the mid-twentieth century. Imitating humans was the first goal, with many false starts (simulating the brain, copying human logic, drawing on animals). By the turn of the Millennium, statistical machine learning became the gold standard, and AI grew as the Internet exploded.
- **Beating humans (C):** AI beat chess grandmasters and won video game tournaments, and could simulate all possible outcomes to pick the best one. Early job worries focused on repetitive tasks, but generative AI made it seem that no profession is safe.
- **Generative AI and plagiarism (D):** Generative AI uses colossal amounts of data, fed in or scraped from the Internet, to produce "original" content (emails, essays, songs, paintings) pieced together from millions of human creations. Some say humans are also inspired by others; critics demand legislation and call tools like Midjourney sophisticated plagiarism rather than real intelligence.
- **The bigger threat: AGI (E):** Limited to "input-led theft", AI may not be an existential threat, and laws could curtail its effects. But Hinton says that if AI surpassed us, the gap in intellect could be like that between a human and a frog. Training new chatbots on chatbot data could produce artificial general intelligence (AGI) that becomes exponentially more intelligent, with goals that may not align with humanity's. We haven't reached that point yet, so the article suggests pausing to evaluate what future we want.

### Key vocabulary
**Listening vocabulary**
- **accustomed to:** familiar with something and accepting it as normal
- **disruptive:** new and original, causing major changes to how something is done
- **guard rail:** a safety measure to prevent something bad happening
- **explosive:** increasing suddenly and rapidly
- **reactive:** showing a reaction or response
- **accusatory:** suggesting someone has done something wrong
- **nightmare scenario:** the worst thing that could happen
- **stake:** something you risk losing
- **open a Pandora's box:** to start something that causes many unforeseen problems

**Reading vocabulary**
- **renowned:** famous and respected
- **colossal:** extremely large
- **treaty:** a formal agreement between two or more countries
- **exponentially:** in a way that becomes faster and faster
- **prominently:** in a way that gives something a lot of attention
- **curtail:** limit something or make it last for a shorter time
- **advent:** the coming of an important event, person or invention
- **array:** a large or impressive group or collection
- **trepidation:** great worry or fear about something unpleasant that may happen
- **plea:** a serious emotional request, especially for something needing action now

**Expressions and phrasal verbs**
- **the gold standard:** a high level of quality that others try to copy
- **thick and fast:** quickly and in large quantities
- **piece something together:** put separate parts together to make a complete whole
- **rail against:** complain about something in a very angry way
- **a/the bright idea:** a clever idea, often used in a joking way to mean a very stupid one
